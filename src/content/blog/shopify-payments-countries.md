# Shopify Payments Countries: Full 2026 Eligibility List

Shopify Payments lets you accept credit cards directly through your Shopify store without connecting a third-party gateway. But it's only available in specific countries. This guide covers every supported country in 2026, eligibility rules, currency details, and what to do if your country isn't on the list.

## What Is Shopify Payments?

Shopify Payments is Shopify's built-in payment processor, powered by Stripe on the backend. Because it's native to the platform, you skip the extra step of connecting an external gateway like Authorize.net or Braintree.

The biggest financial benefit: Shopify waives the 0.5%–2% transaction fee it normally charges when you use third-party gateways. You still pay credit card processing rates, but removing that extra layer of fees adds up fast.

Shopify Payments also unlocks Shop Pay (Shopify's accelerated checkout), buy-now-pay-later installment options, and integrated fraud analysis. Every transaction is covered under PCI DSS compliance, so you don't manage security certifications yourself. (Source: Shopify, 2026)

**Example:** A US-based apparel brand processing $80,000/month through a third-party gateway on the Basic plan pays $1,600/month in Shopify transaction fees alone (2%). Switching to Shopify Payments eliminates that $1,600 entirely.

## Eligibility Requirements by Country

Having your business in a supported country is just the first step. You also need to meet several verification requirements before Shopify Payments activates on your store.

**Core requirements across all countries:**

- A Shopify store on a paid plan (Basic, Shopify, or Advanced—Starter plan doesn't qualify)
- A bank account domiciled in the same supported country as your business
- A government-issued photo ID for identity verification
- A valid business registration or tax identification number

In the United States, Shopify requires your Social Security Number (SSN) for sole proprietors or an Employer Identification Number (EIN) for LLCs and corporations. Non-US countries require the local equivalent—ABN in Australia, UTR in the UK, or a VAT number in most EU countries. (Source: Shopify, 2026)

Some EU countries require active VAT registration before you can access all Shopify Payments features, including cross-border selling tools through Shopify Markets. Business type restrictions also vary: Germany supports GmbH and sole traders, while Hong Kong SAR requires a limited company or sole proprietorship.

## Supported Currencies and Multi-Currency Selling

Shopify Payments supports over 130 presentment currencies when paired with Shopify Markets. A presentment currency is what your customer sees at checkout—so a buyer in Tokyo sees JPY while a buyer in London sees GBP, regardless of where your store is based. (Source: Shopify, 2026)

Your payout currency is always your store's home currency. If you're a US-based store and a customer pays in EUR, Shopify converts that payment to USD before depositing it in your bank account. A currency conversion fee of 1.5% applies to these foreign-currency transactions on most plans. On the Advanced plan, some merchants negotiate slightly lower conversion rates.

**Key distinction:** Switzerland pays out in CHF, Japan in JPY, and most EU countries in EUR. If your bank account currency doesn't match the payout currency for your country, you won't be able to activate Shopify Payments.

**Example:** A Canadian store selling to US customers displays prices in USD through Shopify Markets. When a $100 USD order comes in, Shopify converts it to CAD at the current exchange rate, deducts the 1.5% conversion fee ($1.50 USD equivalent), and deposits the remainder in the merchant's Canadian bank account.

## Countries Where Shopify Payments Is NOT Available

Several major e-commerce markets still don't support Shopify Payments in 2026. The most notable absences include India, Brazil, Mexico, the United Arab Emirates, South Africa, Pakistan, Thailand, Indonesia, and the Philippines.

The reasons vary. In some cases, local banking regulations restrict how international payment processors operate. In others, Stripe (which powers Shopify Payments) hasn't expanded its infrastructure to the region. Fraud risk thresholds and anti-money-laundering compliance also play a role in Shopify's country-by-country rollout decisions. (Source: Stripe Global Availability, 2026)

If your business is in an unsupported country, you must use a third-party payment gateway and pay the additional 0.5%–2% Shopify transaction fee on every order. Recommended alternatives include Razorpay for India, Mercado Pago for Latin America, and PayPal for broad global coverage.

**Example:** A Shopify merchant in Mumbai using Razorpay on the Basic plan pays Razorpay's processing fee *plus* Shopify's 2% transaction fee on each sale. On $20,000/month in revenue, that's an extra $400/month compared to what a US merchant with Shopify Payments would pay.

## Best Alternatives If Your Country Isn't Supported

**PayPal** remains the most widely accessible option, operating in 200+ countries and territories. Most Shopify merchants in unsupported regions start here because of buyer trust and fast setup. (Source: PayPal, 2026)

**Stripe direct integration** is available in more countries than Shopify Payments itself. If Stripe operates in your country but Shopify Payments doesn't, you can still connect Stripe as a third-party gateway—though you'll pay Shopify's transaction fee on top of Stripe's processing rate.

**Regional gateways** often offer better local payment method coverage. Razorpay supports UPI and Indian bank transfers. Mercado Pago covers installment payments popular across Brazil, Argentina, and Mexico. 2Checkout (now Verifone) handles cross-border selling for merchants in 200+ countries.

**The US LLC route:** Some merchants in unsupported countries form a US-based LLC (often in Delaware or Wyoming), open a US bank account, and qualify for Shopify Payments that way. This is legal, but it comes with US tax filing obligations, potential double taxation, and ongoing compliance costs. Consult an international tax professional before pursuing this path.

Shopify supports over 100 payment gateways across different countries. Check **Settings > Payments > Third-party providers** in your admin to see which gateways are available for your specific region.

## How to Activate Shopify Payments in Your Country

Activation takes about five minutes if you have your documents ready. Here's the exact process:

1. **Go to Settings > Payments** in your Shopify admin dashboard
2. **Click "Activate Shopify Payments"** — if this button is greyed out, your country or plan doesn't qualify
3. **Enter your business information:** legal name, address, business type, and tax ID
4. **Add your banking details:** account number, routing number (US), sort code (UK), or equivalent
5. **Submit identity verification:** upload a government-issued photo ID
6. **Set your payout schedule:** choose daily, weekly, or monthly deposits

Verification is instant for most merchants. New businesses or those in higher-risk product categories may wait 2–3 business days. (Source: Shopify, 2026)

**Pro tip:** Complete your store's legal pages (refund policy, terms of service, shipping policy) and add a valid phone number before activating. Incomplete store profiles are more likely to trigger verification holds.

## Common Issues and How to Fix Them

**Account on hold:** This usually happens when Shopify flags your product category as higher-risk (supplements, CBD, dropshipping with long shipping times) or your verification documents don't match your business registration. Contact Shopify Support with your business license and ID to resolve it.

**Payout delays:** New accounts may experience a rolling reserve where Shopify holds a percentage of your revenue for a set period. This is standard fraud protection and typically lifts after 90 days of consistent processing history.

**Activation button greyed out:** Check three things—your store's registered country matches your bank account country, you're on the Basic plan or higher (not Starter), and your business type is supported in your region.

**Chargebacks:** Shopify Payments charges a $15 dispute fee per chargeback in the US (fees vary by country). You can submit evidence through your Shopify admin, and if you win, the fee is refunded. Shopify provides a guided workflow for responding to disputes within the required timeframe. (Source: Shopify, 2026)

## FAQ

### Which countries support Shopify Payments in 2026?
As of 2026, Shopify Payments is available in 23 countries including the US, Canada, UK, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Japan, Hong Kong SAR, and most of Western Europe. Shopify occasionally adds new countries, so check your Shopify admin for the latest.

### Can I use Shopify Payments if I live in an unsupported country?
Not directly. Your business must be legally registered and have a bank account in a supported country. Some merchants form a US LLC to qualify, but consult a legal or tax professional before doing this.

### Does Shopify Payments support multiple currencies?
Yes. With Shopify Markets, you can display prices in 130+ currencies. Your payouts still arrive in your home currency, and a conversion fee (typically 1.5%) applies to foreign-currency transactions.

### Why is the Shopify Payments option greyed out in my admin?
This usually means your store's country doesn't match your bank account country, you're on the Starter or trial plan, or your business type isn't supported. Check **Settings > Payments** for a specific error message.

### Will Shopify charge transaction fees if I don't use Shopify Payments?
Yes. If you use a third-party payment gateway, Shopify charges 0.5% to 2% per transaction depending on your plan. Using Shopify Payments removes these fees entirely.

### Is Shopify Payments available in India?
No, not as of 2026. Indian merchants should use gateways like Razorpay, PayU, or PayPal, which are compatible with Shopify. You'll pay the standard third-party transaction fee on each order.

### How long does Shopify Payments verification take?
Most accounts are verified instantly. In some cases, especially for new businesses or higher-risk categories, verification can take 2–3 business days. You'll receive an email once approved.
